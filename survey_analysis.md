# Follow-Up Survey Analysis

Analysis of [survey_results.csv](supporting_materials/prioritization/survey_results.csv) — 26 responses to
the ranking survey sent via
[survey_message.md](supporting_materials/prioritization/survey_message.md) following the PAC AI strategy
retreat. Source data is raw survey export; not hand-edited.

---

## Priority actions by outcome

Respondents picked their top TWO actions within each of the three outcome areas.
Counts are out of 26 respondents (each contributes up to 2 picks per area).

### Ensure AI Literacy for All Tarleton Graduates

| Action | Votes |
|---|---|
| Integrate AI learning experiences into existing academic programs | 19 |
| Define core AI competencies every Tarleton graduate should possess | 19 |
| Create AI-related projects, internships, and experiential learning opportunities | 6 |
| Launch AI certificates and microcredentials | 4 |
| Expand industry partnerships focused on AI workforce skills | 4 |

Strong, near-unanimous consensus on two actions — integrating AI into existing
coursework and defining core competencies — well ahead of certificates/partnerships.

### Accelerate Student Retention and Success Through AI

| Action | Votes |
|---|---|
| AI-enhanced advising and degree planning | 15 |
| Early-alert systems for at-risk students | 12 |
| Career readiness and job placement support | 8 |
| Improved student service and issue resolution | 8 |
| AI-powered tutoring and academic support | 7 |
| Personalized student communications | 2 |

Advising/degree-planning and early-alert clearly lead.

### Enhance Service and Operations Through AI

| Action | Votes |
|---|---|
| Streamline business processes and automate routine tasks | 14 |
| Enhance data-informed decision-making | 11 |
| Improve service speed and responsiveness | 10 |
| Increase employee effectiveness through AI-enabled tools | 8 |
| Improve access to institutional knowledge | 8 |

## Foundational enabler needing the most immediate attention

| Enabler | Votes |
|---|---|
| Equip faculty and staff | 12 |
| Data governance, safety, and guardrails | 10 |
| Resource AI initiatives adequately | 4 |

Split almost evenly between "train our people" and "put guardrails in place first,"
together accounting for 22 of 26 responses (85%). Resourcing/funding was a distant
third.

Keep perspective on resourcing's last-place finish — nothing they voted for happens without adequate resources.

If the question had instead been "Which road-trip enabler needs the most immediate attention: A) book hotel, B) choose activities, C) get fuel," A and B would win every time — those are the things people get excited about. But nothing happens without C. Resourcing placing last here is neither surprising nor meaningful.

## AI ambition spectrum placement

Where respondents think Tarleton should aim (per the slide-3 spectrum referenced in
`supporting_materials/prioritization/survey_message.md`; "AI-intergrated" in the source data is a typo for
"AI-integrated," normalized here):

| Position | Votes |
|---|---|
| AI-integrated (4th of 6) | 13 |
| AI-centric (5th of 6) | 7 |
| AI-augmented (3rd of 6) | 6 |

Half of respondents want AI "integrated into core processes" (the middle of the
6-point spectrum); nobody selected the two extremes (Not AI-enabled/AI-assisted, or
AI-native). Ambition clusters solidly in the middle-to-upper range.

## If Tarleton could achieve only ONE major AI outcome

26 free-text responses. No single dominant theme, but they cluster into three groups:

**Vision, policy, and governance first (~9 responses)** — e.g. "Define a longterm
philosophy on AI integration," "A clear, practical AI-use policy," "Shared
institutional definitions around appropriate and ethical use of AI," "Establish a
clear, ethics-centered institutional framework." A meaningful share of respondents
believe the single highest-leverage outcome isn't a tool or a training program, but an
agreed-upon institutional stance on what responsible AI use means at Tarleton.

**Equip people (~7 responses)** — e.g. "Equip the faculty and staff. Everything else
hinges on sound and effective decision making," "All faculty/staff are AI literate,"
"Every Tarleton graduate leaves AI-ready."

**Concrete student-facing tools (~7 responses)** — early-alert for at-risk students
(mentioned twice, independently), AI-enhanced advising, a unified "what-if" academic
progress view, transcript/degree-audit processing.

**Operational (~3 responses)** — service/operations broadly, records processing,
Canvas-embedded AI.

## Open suggestions and challenges

14 respondents left additional comments (12 blank). Recurring themes, roughly in order
of frequency:

1. **Treat this as institutional/organizational change, not a technology project.**
   Multiple respondents (independently) used nearly this exact framing — clear
   leadership, governance, faculty development, and measurable outcomes matter more
   than picking tools.
2. **We need one shared, unified vision — not everyone "running a different race."**
   Several comments worry that without a common definition of responsible AI use,
   different units will diverge in ways that are hard to reconcile later.
3. **Policy will go stale fast; build in a review cadence.** AI is moving quickly
   enough that a one-time policy won't hold up — plan for regular revisiting.
4. **Training/equipping people is foundational, but must build ownership, not
   dependence.** Two lengthy, related comments (possibly the same respondent
   resubmitting) stress that AI should assist, not replace, disciplined human judgment
   — "we have to own the technology, not vice versa."
5. **Data privacy, security, and governance concerns**, including one specific
   caution that specialized AI agents can't be adopted yet until data-leakage /
   operational-exposure risks are mitigated.
6. **Skepticism about current output quality and timelines** — one respondent noted
   months spent building on validated/governed data with output still not
   production-ready, flagging that build timelines may be longer than expected.
7. One respondent objected to the term "AI-centric," preferring "student-centric"
   framing — AI as a tool in service of the student experience, not a goal in itself.

---

## Takeaways for prioritization

- The literacy and advising/early-alert priorities are unambiguous — near-consensus,
  not close calls. These are strong candidates for early roadmap milestones.
- The "ONE major outcome" and open-suggestions responses converge on the same message
  from a different angle: before any specific tool or training rollout, define and
  communicate a shared institutional stance on responsible AI use. This is the
  single most-repeated idea across the whole survey, independent of how respondents
  answered the structured questions.
- Nobody wants Tarleton at either extreme of the ambition spectrum — treat "AI-native"
  as out of scope for near-term planning, and don't undersell ambition either (nobody
  chose "Not AI-enabled" or "AI-assisted").
